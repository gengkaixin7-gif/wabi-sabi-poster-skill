# English Detailed: Wabi-Sabi Product Story

## Intent

Combine a believable human–product interaction with enough verified information to support a purchase. Keep the hierarchy quiet and editorial: product first, then theme, core benefits, and transaction details. Detail must come from disciplined typography rather than interface-like cards.

## Content planning

- Extract the exact brand, full product name, specifications, 2–4 strongest verified benefits, price, currency, date range, conditions, and call to action.
- Use the user's English copy when available. Otherwise translate faithfully without adding claims.
- Missing optional fields may be omitted. If a required price, date, or condition is absent, ask only for that field while continuing visual planning.

## Human and product

- Choose a real use gesture, quiet companionship, or restrained symbolic scale. The pose must explain handling or emotional context without turning the person into the main subject.
- Preserve real structure, color, logo placement, and scale cues. Fingers, handles, cables, openings, reflections, and ground contact need special inspection.
- Use natural fabrics and surfaces that fit the category. Do not imply handmade, organic, antique, sustainable, or Japanese origin unless the source material supports it.

## Information hierarchy

1. Product and emotional headline.
2. Brand and full product name.
3. One primary benefit or number.
4. Two or three secondary verified benefits.
5. Price, date, conditions, and call to action when supplied.

- Use a narrow text column, a quiet bottom ledger, or two asymmetrical information groups. Keep generous gaps between groups.
- Use small monochrome line icons only when they improve scanning; all icons must share stroke and visual weight. Do not place every item in a rounded card.
- Price can be prominent but should not become a red discount badge. Keep currency, unit, “from,” coupon, and eligibility terms adjacent to the number.
- Dates and conditions remain readable and cannot be hidden in decorative microtype.
- A call to action may be plain text, an underline, or a small solid block. Avoid simulated app buttons unless the delivery format requires one.

## Visual character

- Favor matte surfaces, paper or fabric texture, limewash, old wood, stone, clay, oxidized metal, and soft directional daylight.
- Keep one muted product-derived accent. Let typography be charcoal, warm black, bone, or another low-saturation high-contrast color.
- Use subtle grain and faded ink sparingly. Do not degrade product photography or exact transactional text.

## Check

- Product remains dominant; the person supports a credible story.
- Full product name and all required sales facts are present and accurate.
- Information is easy to scan without a card grid or promotional sticker collage.
- Natural texture and age cues are restrained, clean, and relevant to the product.
- Headline, product, price, and secondary details have distinct weights at full size and phone size.
